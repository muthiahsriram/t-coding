# HDB concessionary loan

The HDB concessionary loan rate is pegged at 0.1 percentage points above the CPF Ordinary Account interest rate, giving 2.6% per annum while the Ordinary Account floor stands at 2.5%. The rate is reviewed quarterly and has not moved for many years, which makes it unusually predictable compared with a floating bank loan.

The Loan-to-Value (LTV) limit for an HDB concessionary loan is 75% of the purchase price or value, whichever is lower, reduced from 80% in August 2024. The buyer must fund the remaining 25%, of which at least 5% must be in cash for a bank loan; an HDB loan permits the full downpayment from the Ordinary Account.

# Bank loans

Bank loans for residential property are subject to an LTV limit of 75% where the borrower has no outstanding residential mortgage, falling to 45% for a second concurrent mortgage and 35% for a third. At least 5% of the purchase price must be paid in cash on a first mortgage, rising to 25% on subsequent ones.

Bank rates are typically pegged to a floating benchmark with a spread, or fixed for an initial period of one to five years before reverting to a floating peg. A client comparing 2.6% fixed-by-policy against a two-year fixed bank rate should be shown the reversion rate, not only the teaser.

# Debt servicing limits

The Total Debt Servicing Ratio (TDSR) caps total monthly debt obligations at 55% of gross monthly income. It applies to all property loans and counts every other debt obligation, including car loans, personal loans and credit card minimums.

The Mortgage Servicing Ratio (MSR) caps the monthly repayment on an HDB flat or an executive condominium bought from a developer at 30% of gross monthly income. Where both apply, the lower binds.

Both ratios are assessed using a medium-term interest rate floor rather than the actual contracted rate, so an applicant may be assessed at a materially higher rate than they will pay in the first years of the loan.

# Advice implications

Refinancing from an HDB concessionary loan to a bank loan is irreversible: a borrower who takes a bank loan cannot return to the HDB loan. The comparison must therefore be made over the full remaining tenure and not over the fixed-rate teaser period.

A client with surplus cash and an HDB loan at 2.6% faces a genuine question rather than an obvious one. Prepaying the mortgage earns a guaranteed 2.6% after tax; leaving the money in the Ordinary Account earns 2.5% guaranteed; investing it must clear 2.6% after charges with certainty the client can tolerate.
