# Eligibility

A member may invest Ordinary Account savings under CPFIS-OA only after setting aside the first S$20,000 in the Ordinary Account. A member may invest Special Account savings under CPFIS-SA only after setting aside the first S$40,000 in the Special Account. The member must be at least 18, not an undischarged bankrupt, and must have completed the CPFIS Self-Awareness Questionnaire before making a first investment.

The Self-Awareness Questionnaire is a knowledge check, not a suitability assessment. Passing it does not discharge the adviser's own suitability obligations.

# Instrument limits

Within CPFIS-OA, no more than 35% of investible savings may be placed in shares, property funds and corporate bonds taken together. No more than 10% of investible savings may be placed in gold or gold-related products. Investible savings means Ordinary Account balance plus amounts already withdrawn for investment and education.

CPFIS-SA carries a narrower approved list. Higher-risk instruments admitted under CPFIS-OA, including individual equities and gold, are not admitted under CPFIS-SA.

# The hurdle rate

Investments made with Ordinary Account savings must be judged against the 2.5% per annum guaranteed floor those savings would otherwise earn, and Special Account savings against 4.0%. These are risk-free, government-guaranteed rates. An investment expected to return 3% with equity-like volatility is a poor trade against a 2.5% floor and an unacceptable one against a 4.0% floor.

Historically a majority of CPFIS-OA participants have failed to beat the Ordinary Account floor rate after charges. Advisers must state the hurdle rate explicitly in any CPFIS recommendation, and must not present a CPFIS investment return without comparing it to the account rate it displaces.

# Product charges

Sales charges on CPFIS-included unit trusts and investment-linked policies are capped, and wrap fee arrangements are permitted only where disclosed. Charges are deducted before the member's return, so a 1.5% annual charge against a 2.5% hurdle consumes most of the expected excess return of a conservative portfolio.
