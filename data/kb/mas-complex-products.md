# Specified Investment Products

A Specified Investment Product is one whose terms, features or risks are not generally well understood by retail investors. The category includes structured notes, structured deposits, futures, contracts for difference, certain exchange traded funds using derivatives or leverage, and exchange traded notes.

Before a retail customer may transact in a Specified Investment Product, the intermediary must conduct a Customer Knowledge Assessment for unlisted products or a Customer Account Review for listed ones. The assessment tests educational qualifications, relevant work experience and prior transaction experience.

A customer who does not have the relevant knowledge or experience may still proceed only after receiving advice, and the intermediary must document that advice. Simply obtaining a signed acknowledgement of risk does not satisfy the requirement.

# Excluded Investment Products

Ordinary shares, plain vanilla bonds meeting prescribed criteria, most non-derivative exchange traded funds, unit trusts that are not structured, and Singapore Government Securities are Excluded Investment Products. They do not require a Customer Knowledge Assessment.

Classification is determined by the product's structure, not by its volatility. A broad equity index fund is an Excluded Investment Product even though it can fall sharply, while a capital-protected structured deposit is a Specified Investment Product even though it can be described as conservative.

# Suitability

A recommendation must have a reasonable basis. The intermediary must gather the customer's financial objectives, risk tolerance, employment status, financial situation, investment experience and, where relevant, their existing investment portfolio, and must document why the recommendation fits.

Where a customer declines to provide the information, the intermediary must document the refusal and may not represent the transaction as advised. Where a recommendation is not suitable but the customer insists, the transaction must be recorded as execution-only with the mismatch documented.

# Fair dealing and disclosure

Fair Dealing Guidelines require that customers are offered products suitable to their circumstances, receive clear and adequate information, and are served by competent representatives. Remuneration structures must not incentivise unsuitable recommendations.

All material charges, including sales charges, platform fees, trailer fees and any foreign exchange spread applied on conversion, must be disclosed in a form the customer can compare. A return quoted before charges is not a disclosure.
