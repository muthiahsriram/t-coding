# Contributions

The Supplementary Retirement Scheme is voluntary. The annual contribution cap is S$15,300 for Singapore Citizens and Permanent Residents, and S$35,700 for foreigners. Contributions must be made by 31 December to count toward that year's tax relief.

Every dollar contributed reduces chargeable income dollar for dollar, subject to the overall personal income tax relief cap of S$80,000 per Year of Assessment. The value of the relief therefore depends on the client's marginal rate: at a 7% marginal rate a full contribution saves about S$1,071, while at a 22% marginal rate it saves about S$3,366. Recommending a maximum SRS contribution to a client whose marginal rate is 2% is close to pointless and locks up the money for decades.

Contributions sit in an SRS account as cash earning a negligible interest rate until invested. Uninvested SRS cash loses real value to inflation, and an unused SRS balance is one of the most common findings in a portfolio review.

# Withdrawals

The statutory retirement age that governs SRS withdrawal is the one prevailing at the time of the member's first contribution. Withdrawals on or after that age may be spread over a maximum of ten years, and only 50% of each withdrawal is subject to tax.

A withdrawal before the statutory retirement age is fully taxable and attracts a 5% penalty. Limited exceptions apply, including death, medical grounds, terminal illness, bankruptcy and full withdrawal by a foreigner who has maintained the account for at least ten years.

Spreading withdrawals across the full ten-year window is usually the difference between paying nothing and paying a material tax bill, because each year's 50% taxable portion can often be kept inside the zero-rate or lowest-rate bands.

# Investing SRS funds

SRS funds may be invested in shares, bonds, unit trusts, fixed deposits, Singapore Government Securities and single-premium insurance products. There is no CPFIS-style percentage cap on instrument type.

Because SRS money is locked until retirement age, it has the longest horizon of any account most clients hold. Holding it in cash or short-dated deposits is a mismatch between the account's horizon and its allocation.
