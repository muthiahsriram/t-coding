# Rates and bands

Singapore taxes resident individuals on a progressive scale. The first S$20,000 of chargeable income is not taxed. Rates then step up through the bands, reaching 22% on chargeable income above S$320,000 and 24% at the top band introduced from Year of Assessment 2024.

Because the scale is progressive, the value of any relief equals the relief amount multiplied by the client's marginal rate, not their average rate. A relief that is worth S$3,366 to one client is worth S$306 to another.

# The relief cap

Total personal income tax reliefs are capped at S$80,000 per Year of Assessment. The cap applies to the sum of all reliefs claimed, including earned income relief, spouse and child reliefs, CPF cash top-up relief, SRS relief, CPF relief and course fees relief.

A client already near the cap gains nothing from an additional SRS contribution. Checking headroom against the cap before recommending a contribution is a required step, not an optional one.

# Retirement-related reliefs

Cash top-ups to one's own Special Account or Retirement Account, or to those of family members, attract relief of up to S$8,000 per calendar year for self top-ups and a further S$8,000 for top-ups to family members. MediSave voluntary contributions attract relief subject to the Basic Healthcare Sum and the CPF Annual Limit.

SRS contributions attract relief up to the scheme cap. Unlike a CPF top-up, an SRS contribution remains invested at the member's own direction and is withdrawable, subject to penalty, before retirement age.

# Ordering

Where a client has limited surplus cash and headroom under the cap, the ordering that usually maximises after-tax outcome is: MediSave voluntary contribution where a medical need is foreseeable, then CPF Retirement Account top-up for the guaranteed 4% floor, then SRS for flexibility of investment. This ordering trades liquidity for certainty and should be reversed where the client's emergency reserve is thin.
