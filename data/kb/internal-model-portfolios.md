# The four models

The conservative model holds 30% equity, 60% bonds and 10% commodities, and targets capital preservation with a horizon under five years or a low stated tolerance for loss.

The balanced model holds 60% equity, 35% bonds and 5% commodities. It is the default for clients with a horizon between five and fifteen years and no strong stated preference.

The growth model holds 80% equity, 15% bonds and 5% commodities, for clients with a horizon beyond ten years who have confirmed they can tolerate a peak-to-trough fall of a third.

The aggressive model holds 95% equity and 5% commodities, with no bond allocation. It is appropriate only where the client has a horizon beyond fifteen years, no goal funded from this portfolio inside five years, and documented experience of holding through a prior drawdown.

# Drift and rebalancing

Drift is measured as the absolute difference between the actual weight of an asset class and its model weight. Drift within 5 percentage points requires no action. Drift between 5 and 10 points is reported and rebalanced at the next scheduled review. Drift beyond 10 points triggers a rebalancing proposal within ten business days.

Rebalancing is executed with new contributions before it is executed by selling, because a sale in a taxable account may realise a gain and always incurs a spread and commission. Singapore does not tax capital gains, which removes the largest constraint on rebalancing that applies in other jurisdictions, but transaction costs remain.

# Mandate mismatch

A portfolio whose allocation matches a different model from the client's assessed risk profile is a mandate mismatch and outranks drift as a finding. A conservative client holding the growth allocation is not drifting; they are in the wrong portfolio, and the review must establish whether the risk profile or the portfolio is wrong before proposing trades.

Where a client's stated risk profile has not been reassessed within 24 months, the review must flag the profile itself as stale rather than assume it.

# Ordering of recommendations

Where several actions are indicated, the review presents them in this order: restore the emergency reserve, cure any breach of the concentration limits, correct a mandate mismatch, fund goals that are behind, then rebalance drift. Presenting a rebalancing trade above an unfunded near-term goal inverts the client's actual priorities.
