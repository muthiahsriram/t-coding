# Buyer's Stamp Duty (BSD)

Buyer's Stamp Duty, abbreviated BSD, is payable on every residential property purchase, on the higher of purchase price and market value. The scale is 1% on the first S$180,000, 2% on the next S$180,000, 3% on the next S$640,000, 4% on the next S$500,000, 5% on the next S$1,500,000 and 6% on any amount above S$3,000,000.

Buyer's Stamp Duty is payable within 14 days of the date of the document. It cannot be financed by the mortgage and must come from cash or, in permitted cases, the Ordinary Account.

# Additional Buyer's Stamp Duty (ABSD)

Additional Buyer's Stamp Duty, abbreviated ABSD, depends on the buyer's profile and the number of residential properties they will own after the purchase.

Singapore Citizens pay nothing on a first residential property, 20% on a second and 30% on a third or subsequent property. Permanent Residents pay 5% on a first property, 30% on a second and 35% on a third or subsequent. Foreigners pay 60% on any residential property. Entities pay 65%, and housing developers pay 35% plus a non-remittable 5%.

Nationals of the United States, Switzerland, Liechtenstein, Norway and Iceland are accorded the same treatment as Singapore Citizens under free trade agreements.

# Remission on a matrimonial home

A married couple with at least one Singapore Citizen spouse may apply for remission of ABSD paid on a replacement matrimonial home, provided the first property is sold within six months of the purchase of a completed replacement, or within six months of the issue of the Temporary Occupation Permit for an uncompleted one. The application must be made within six months of the sale.

# Advice implications

Additional Buyer's Stamp Duty on a second property is frequently the single largest cash outlay in a client's plan and is routinely omitted from client-prepared projections. A second property purchased at S$1,500,000 by a Singapore Citizen attracts S$300,000 in ABSD on top of S$44,600 in BSD, none of which can be borrowed.

Any goal described as a second property, investment property or rental property must have the applicable stamp duty added to its target amount before funding adequacy is assessed.
